Your seven questions, answered

Our starting position on each. The first milestone either confirms it with evidence or changes it.

What can we leverage from Salesforce rather than rebuilding?

Flow, Agentforce actions, list views, reports, the standard consent and contact point objects, Omni-Channel routing where the customer already has it, and the Journey Builder canvas. Rebuild none of them. Your build is the rail and the thin layer that exposes it.

Which capabilities should be Salesforce-native components?

Everything a Salesforce user touches daily: the conversation panel on any record, bulk send from list views, invocable actions for Flow and Agentforce, consent sync, and delivery status written back to the record.

Which capabilities should live in our own platform?

Number provisioning, 10DLC brand and campaign registration, opt-out enforcement, content screening, the RCS template registry, delivery analytics and the AI agent runtime. Your public API already covers registrations, opt-out enforcement, idempotent requests and webhooks, so the package calls those rather than copying them.

Standalone RCS campaign builder, embedded, or hybrid?

Hybrid. Compose and register RCS templates once on your platform, because sender verification and template approval are rail work. Render the same composer inside Salesforce and expose saved templates to Flow and Journey Builder. A standalone builder adds another login. A fully native builder means rebuilding it for each cloud.

How do enterprises use our CPaaS instead of Salesforce’s providers?

Two doors on one rail. The package door works in any Sales or Service org with no messaging add-on. The enterprise door is a Bring Your Own Channel connector, so customers who already pay for Digital Engagement see your SMS and RCS inside the Service console and Omni-Channel. Same numbers, same consent and the same bill on both.

How do we cut implementation complexity?

Ship the connected app, platform event and permission sets inside the package so the admin runs one guided setup screen, then registers the 10DLC brand and campaign from inside Salesforce. The bar to beat is the manual list in today’s partner messaging guides. The target is install to first compliant message in one working session.

What would make an enterprise choose us over Vonage?

Vonage already offers messaging to any Salesforce user without a per-user licence, so native coverage alone will not win. Our bet is three things together: your price (your own comparison puts you up to 78% under Vonage), compliance enforced at the rail so a message that breaks opt-out or content rules never reaches a carrier whichever Salesforce surface sent it, and self-serve from install to first message to match your product-led motion. The teardown tests whether that gap holds in practice.

Three assumptions worth challenging

You asked for someone who pushes back. These are the three we would raise first.

Start with Bring Your Own Channel.

It only works for customers who already pay for Digital Engagement with the Partner Messaging add-on. Start with the package door, which works in any Sales or Service org, and bring the connector in for enterprise deals.

Build a standalone RCS campaign builder.

It adds a login and pulls campaigns away from the CRM record. Build the composer once on your platform and embed it where people already work.

Marketing Cloud is the first market.

It is the most crowded door. Salesforce ships its own SMS, WhatsApp and RCS in Marketing Cloud Next, and Vonage and Sinch already sell into Journey Builder. Your SMB and mid-market base is far more likely to live on record pages and in Flow.
